About area codes (NANP)

The North American Numbering Plan covers the United States, Canada, and much of the Caribbean under a single dialing plan.

One plan, 25+ regions

The North American Numbering Plan (NANP) covers the United States, Canada, and many Caribbean countries. Area codes route calls to specific geographic regions or to toll-free services.

Overlays vs. splits

Overlays add a new code to the same region (e.g., 212 overlaid by 332 and 646). Splits carve a region into two or more unique codes. Overlays are now far more common than splits.

Dialing patterns

Most regions now require 10-digit dialing (area code + local number). Toll-free numbers are dialed the same way and may originate from any NANP country.

Frequently asked questions

How do I find which city an area code belongs to?

Use the search box above to enter the code (e.g., 415) or a city or state name. Click a result to see a list of covered cities and overlays.

What does “overlay” mean?

An overlay introduces one or more new area codes for the same geographic region to prevent number exhaustion — residents keep their current numbers while new numbers get the new codes.

Are toll-free numbers tied to a location?

No. Toll-free area codes (like 800 and 888) route to businesses and services and aren't tied to a particular city or state.

How many area codes are there in the United States?

The North American Numbering Plan currently includes 348 geographic area codes and 7 toll-free codes covering the United States, its territories, Canada, and much of the Caribbean.

Can I buy a phone number in a specific area code?

Yes. Every area code in this directory links to live PhoneNumbers.com inventory — local, vanity, and toll-free numbers that activate immediately after checkout.

Why does my region have more than one area code?

High-growth regions exhaust their numbering supply, so NANP administrators add overlay codes to the same territory. Existing numbers keep their code; new numbers are issued under the overlay, and 10-digit dialing becomes standard.
