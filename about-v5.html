The problem

Most businesses never choose their number

Open an account and a carrier hands you whatever it has free that day. Seven digits, chosen by nobody, that you will print on trucks and business cards for the next decade.

Nobody would pick a company name that way. Or a domain. Yet the number goes on every invoice, every van, every ad, and it is usually the only thing a customer has to remember when they finally decide to call.

The good ones are scarce, and they are scarce for a structural reason. An area code holds roughly 7.9 million numbers, and fewer than 800 of them end in 0000. One per exchange. That is why a carrier so rarely has one to offer.

PhoneNumbers.com exists to make those numbers findable.

Why us

Thirty years inside the system that issues them

Phone numbers are not created by websites. They come from carriers, exchange codes and a numbering plan most people never see, governed by rules that decide what is even possible.

We have worked inside that system since 1994, through paging, through wireless, and through the telecommunications infrastructure businesses still run on. That is the difference between listing numbers and understanding where they come from.

The marketplace came later. It exists because three decades of watching businesses accept whatever they were handed made the gap impossible to ignore.
